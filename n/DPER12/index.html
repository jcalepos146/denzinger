Benedict XVI · 2005–2013

DPER 12 Techniques for assisting fertility

[From the Instruction of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith on certain bioethical questions, "Dignitas Personae," Sept. 8, 2008]

Doctrine of the FaithEditorial grade · Authoritative teaching

With regard to the treatment of infertility, new medical techniques must respect three fundamental goods: a) the right to life and to physical integrity of every human being from conception to natural death; b) the unity of marriage, which means reciprocal respect for the right within marriage to become a father or mother only together with the other spouse; c) the specifically human values of sexuality which require “that the procreation of a human person be brought about as the fruit of the conjugal act specific to the love between spouses”. Techniques which assist procreation “are not to be rejected on the grounds that they are artificial. As such, they bear witness to the possibilities of the art of medicine. But they must be given a moral evaluation in reference to the dignity of the human person, who is called to realize his vocation from God to the gift of love and the gift of life”.

In light of this principle, all techniques of heterologous artificial fertilization, as well as those techniques of homologous artificial fertilization which substitute for the conjugal act, are to be excluded. On the other hand, techniques which act as an aid to the conjugal act and its fertility are permitted. The Instruction Donum vitae states: “The doctor is at the service of persons and of human procreation. He does not have the authority to dispose of them or to decide their fate. A medical intervention respects the dignity of persons when it seeks to assist the conjugal act either in order to facilitate its performance or in order to enable it to achieve its objective once it has been normally performed”. And, with regard to homologous artificial insemination, it states: “Homologous artificial insemination within marriage cannot be admitted except for those cases in which the technical means is not a substitute for the conjugal act, but serves to facilitate and to help so that the act attains its natural purpose”.

Editorial assessment · Authoritative teaching (DPER 4–DPER 34). The embryo has the dignity of a person from conception; in vitro fertilization, the freezing of embryos, the destruction of embryos and preimplantation diagnosis are excluded; on cell lines of illicit origin, a duty to avoid them that admits of graver reasons.

Source and coverage: Dignitas Personae · unreviewed locally. Passage grades are editorial assessments.
