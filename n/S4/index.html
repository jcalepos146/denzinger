The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Letter to the Church of Bamberg (1047)

Supplement S4 Clement II: the three expelled and his own election

[Pope Clement II, letter to the Church of Bamberg, in J. von Pflugk-Harttung, Acta Pontificum Romanorum Inedita II, n. 103, p. 68. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

For that head of the world, the Roman See, was faltering through a heretical sickness. The presence of our dearest son, lord Henry, Augustus and emperor, kept watch and pressed on to drive out this sickness, once those three had been expelled [Benedict IX, Sylvester III, Gregory VI] to whom plunder had given the name of pope. Among so many companies of holy fathers who were present, the favor of heavenly grace willed that our most unworthy mediocrity, resisting with all its strength, be chosen to act in the place of the most high prince of the Apostles.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. The new pope's own account, a few weeks after the synod: the three claimants were expelled, and he calls their claim to the name of pope the fruit of plunder. It treats the removal of Gregory VI as removing a simoniac, not as judging a lawful pope.
