Council of Florence: decrees not in Denzinger · 1439–1445 · Session 14, Rome, 7 August 1445

Supplement F2 Benedictus sit Deus: the bull of union with the Chaldeans and Maronites of Cyprus

[Bull of Eugene IV in the last session of the council, at the Lateran. Translated for this site from the Latin of Conciliorum Oecumenicorum Decreta as given by Documenta Catholica Omnia; the closing clauses are abridged there as here. Compare a critical edition before citing.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Profession of faith

Eugene, bishop, servant of the servants of God, for an everlasting record. Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and the God of all consolation [2 Cor. 1:3], who day by day follows with many great favours, and accompanies with happy results beyond our deserving, the vows and pious desires by which, as the duty of our pastoral office requires, we long for the salvation of the Christian people and foster it by continual works, as far as is granted us from on high.

After the union of the Eastern Church with the Western celebrated in the ecumenical council of Florence, and after the return of the Armenians, the Jacobites and the peoples of Mesopotamia, we sent our venerable brother Andrew, archbishop of Colossi, to the regions of the East and the island of Cyprus. He was to confirm in the faith they had accepted the Greeks, Armenians and Jacobites living there, by his preaching and by explaining and declaring the decrees issued for their union and return; and to try, by our admonitions and exhortations, to bring back to the truth of faith those he found estranged from true doctrine in other sects, followers both of Nestorius and of Macarius.

He carried this out most diligently with the wisdom and other virtues with which the Lord, giver of graces, has adorned him. After many and various disputations and discussions he at last removed from their hearts, first, all the impurity of Nestorius, who asserted that Christ was a mere man and that the most blessed Virgin was the mother not of God but of Christ; and then that of the most impious Macarius of Antioch, who, though he professed Christ to be true God and man, yet asserted that there was in him only a divine will and operation, attributing too little to his humanity. With God's assistance he converted to the truth of the orthodox faith our venerable brothers Timothy, metropolitan of the Chaldeans, who in Cyprus have until now been called Nestorians because they followed Nestorius, and Elias, bishop of the Maronites, who with his nation in the same realm was held infected with the dogmas of Macarius, together with the whole multitude of peoples and clergy subject to him in the island of Cyprus. He handed on to these prelates and all their subjects there the faith and teaching which the holy Church has always cherished and kept, and the said prelates received it with the greatest veneration in a large public assembly of the various nations living in that realm, held in the metropolitan church of Saint Sophia.

This done, the Chaldeans sent to us their said metropolitan Timothy, and Elias, bishop of the Maronites, sent an envoy, to make a solemn profession of the faith of the Roman Church, which by the Lord's providence and with the help of blessed Peter the apostle has always remained spotless. In our presence, in this sacred general congregation of the ecumenical council of the Lateran, the metropolitan Timothy reverently and devoutly professed that faith and teaching, first in his own Chaldean tongue, interpreted into Greek and translated from Greek into Latin, as follows:

"I, Timothy, archbishop of Tarsus and metropolitan of the Chaldeans who are in Cyprus, for myself and for all my peoples living in Cyprus, profess, vow and promise to the immortal God, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, and then to you, most holy and most blessed father, Pope Eugene IV, to this holy Apostolic See and to this holy and venerable assembly, that henceforth I shall always remain under obedience to you and your successors and to the holy Roman Church, as to the one mother and head of all the others.

"Also, that henceforth I shall always hold and profess that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son, as the holy Roman Church teaches and holds.

"Also, that henceforth I shall always hold and approve two natures, two wills, one hypostasis and two operations in Christ.

"Also, that henceforth I shall always confess and approve all seven sacraments of the Roman Church, as she holds, teaches and preaches.

"Also, that henceforth I shall never put oil into the holy Eucharist.

"Also, that henceforth I shall always hold, confess, preach and teach whatever the holy Roman Church holds, confesses, teaches and preaches; and whatever she rejects, anathematizes and condemns, I reject, anathematize and condemn, and shall always reject, anathematize and condemn in the future, especially the impieties and blasphemies of the most wicked heresiarch Nestorius, and every other heresy that raises itself against this holy, catholic and apostolic Church.

"This is the faith, holy father, which I vow and promise to hold and keep, and to have held and kept by all those subject to me. I pledge, undertake and promise to deprive of all his goods and benefices anyone who rejects it and rises against it, to excommunicate him and denounce him as a heretic and condemned, and, if he is obstinate, to hand him over degraded to the secular court."

Then our beloved son in Christ Isaac, envoy of our venerable brother Elias, bishop of the Maronites, on his behalf and in his name, made with great veneration a profession like it in every point, rejecting the heresy of Macarius on a single will in Christ.

For the devotion of these professions and the salvation of so many souls we give immense thanks to God and our Lord Jesus Christ, who in our times grants such increase of the faith and bestows such benefits on so many Christian peoples. We receive and approve these professions, and we receive the said metropolitan and bishop in Cyprus and their subjects into the bosom of holy mother Church; and, so long as they remain in the said faith, obedience and devotion, we grant them these graces and privileges. First, that no one shall henceforth dare to call the said metropolitan of the Chaldeans and bishop of the Maronites, their clergy and peoples, or anyone of their people and clergy, heretics, or presume to call the Chaldeans Nestorians.

If anyone shows contempt for this command, we order that he be excommunicated by his ordinary for as long as he delays to make fitting satisfaction, or be fined with some other temporal penalty at the ordinary's discretion.

Also, that the said metropolitan and bishop and their successors shall from now on take precedence in all honours whatever over bishops separated from the communion of the holy Roman Church.

Also, that they may henceforth exercise censures over their subjects, and that those whom they duly excommunicate shall be held by all as excommunicated, and those whom they absolve as absolved.

Also, that the said prelates, priests and clergy may freely celebrate divine services in the churches of Catholics, and Catholics in theirs.

Also, that henceforth the said prelates and clergy, and their lay people of both sexes who have accepted this union and faith, may choose burial in the churches of Catholics, may contract marriage with Catholics, though according to the custom of Latin Catholics, and may enjoy all the benefits, immunities and liberties which other Catholics, lay and clerical, have and enjoy in the said realm.

Let no one therefore … If anyone, however …

Editorial assessment · Profession of faith. A profession of faith made before Eugene IV and the council, and received by him: the procession of the Spirit, two natures and two wills in Christ, the seven sacraments, and the rejection of Nestorius and of Macarius's single will. Its articles restate defined doctrine. The privileges granted at the end (no one may call them heretics or Nestorians; precedence, censures, shared churches, burial and marriage) are discipline.
