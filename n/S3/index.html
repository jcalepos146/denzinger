The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · On the year 1046 (late 11th century)

Supplement S3 The Chronicle of Saint-Bénigne, Dijon: three claimants found simoniacs and invaders

[Anonymous, Chronicon S. Benigni Divionensis, ed. É. Bougaud and J. Garnier (Dijon: Darantière), p. 190. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

In the same year [1046] the aforementioned prince [Henry] went to Rome, and there at that time received the crown of the Empire on the day of the Lord's Nativity by the hand of Pope Clement [II], whom the Emperor himself ordered to be ordained. He also had John [i.e. Gratian, Gregory VI] deposed, who was then presiding on the chair, and Benedict [IX] and Sylvester [III], who in the council held there, their guilt having been examined, were found to be not only simoniacs but even invaders of the Church. At this council were present the lord Archbishop of Lyon himself, and the aforementioned Archbishop Hugh, and several other bishops gathered from various regions.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. A monastic chronicle that names Gregory VI as deposed and Benedict IX and Sylvester III as simoniacs and invaders, and records the bishops present from Burgundy.
