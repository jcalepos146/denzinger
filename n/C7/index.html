Council of Constance: decrees not in Denzinger · 1414–1418 · 1418 and after

Supplement C7 How the popes received Constance

[Summary written for this site.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Juridical act

Martin V confirmed the condemnations of Wycliffe and Hus in the bull Inter Cunctas (see n. 657 ff.).

Gerson reports that Martin V opposed appeals from the Apostolic See in matters of faith and argues against him from Haec Sancta. The date and formal status of the reported text are disputed.

At the last session (22 April 1418) he declared that he approved and ratified what the council had decided conciliariter, as a council, in matters of faith (C9). Whether this covered Haec Sancta is disputed; the usual Catholic reading is that it did not.

He treated the council's acts on the schism as settled: Cossa's deposition was "canonical" (C8), the Pisan pope Alexander V is "our predecessor" in his register (C10), and the next council was forbidden to reopen the depositions or his election (C11). He praised the king of Poland for holding that there is no appeal from the Roman Bishop (C12).

In the 1446 instructions Eugene IV received Constance and Basel up to its transfer, while expressly reserving Peter’s power and the rights of the Apostolic See (B13). In 1447 he expressly received Constance and Frequens, and issued a separate concession on selected Basel decrees for the Germans. Nicholas V confirmed the concessions (B14–B16).

Pius II forbade appeals from the pope to a future council in Exsecrabilis in 1460 (n. 717). His 1463 bull In minoribus agentes honoured Constance and acknowledged the authority of a general council as declared there. He added that he knew of no approved council that had met without papal authority while there was an unquestioned Roman pontiff (B18).

Lateran V affirmed the pope’s authority to convoke, transfer and dissolve councils (n. 740). Vatican I rejected appeals from papal judgments to a council as to a higher authority (n. 1830–1831).
