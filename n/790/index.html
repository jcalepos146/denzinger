Council of Trent · 1545–1563 · Session V (June 17, 1546)

n. 790 Decree On Original Sin

DS 1513Conciliar canonEditorial grade · Defined dogma

3. If anyone asserts that this sin of Adam, which is one in origin and transmitted to all is in each one as his own by propagation, not by imitation, is taken away either by the forces of human nature, or by any remedy other than the merit of the one mediator, our Lord Jesus Christ [see n. 711], who has reconciled us to God in his own blood, "made unto us justice, sanctification, and redemption" [1 Cor. 1:30]; or if he denies that that merit of Jesus Christ is applied to adults as well as to infants by the sacrament of baptism, rightly administered in the form of the Church: let him be anathema. "For there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved..." [Acts 4:12]. Whence that word: "Behold the lamb of God, behold Him who taketh away the sins of the world" [John 1:29]. And that other: "As many of you as have been baptized, have put on Christ" [Gal. 3:27].

Latin text · DS 1513

15133. Si quis hoc Adae peccatum, quod origine unum est et propagatione, non imitatione transfusum omnibus inest unicuique proprium, vel per humanae naturae vires, vel per aliud remedium asserit tolli, quam per meritum unius mediatoris Domini nostri Jesu Christi (cf DS 1340), qui nos Deo reconciliavit in sanguine suo, "factus nobis justitia, sanctificatio et redemptio" (Cor1 1.30); aut negat, ipsum Christi Jesu meritum per baptismi sacramentum, in forma Ecclesiae rite collatum, tam adultis quam parvulis applicari: an. s. Quia "non est aliud nomen sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri" (Act 4.12). Unde illa vox: "Ecce agnus Dei, ecce qui tollit peccata mundi" (cf Jo 1.29). Et illa: "Quicumque baptizati estis, Christum induistis" (Gal 3.27).
