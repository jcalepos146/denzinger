The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Letter to the emperor Henry (before 1049)

Supplement S11 St. Odilo of Cluny: let simoniac heresy be cast out

[St. Odilo of Cluny, letter to the emperor Henry, in Neues Archiv der Gesellschaft für ältere deutsche Geschichtskunde 24, p. 734. Whether it is addressed to St. Henry II or to Henry III is disputed. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

Since you are about to fight the battles of the Lord your God in the first encounter of your apprenticeship in arms, arm yourself with the weapons of justice and clothe yourself in the garments of gladness. Let love of money not corrupt you, and let partiality not turn you from the truth. Let the proud see you as terrible, and let the humble find you gentle. At your coming let the overturned pride of the wicked languish, let the bent-over greed of the arrogant grow numb, and let the afflicted innocence of the poor regain strength. Let the kingdom of Italy rejoice that you have come to it, and let the Roman Empire dance for joy at your arrival. Let bishops, rich and poor, clerics, abbots, monks and all the children of the Church be glad. Let it not be lawful for the greater to oppress the lesser, and let the lesser learn to obey the greater.

Under your government let vices be destroyed, and by your authority let virtues arise. Let simoniac heresy be cast into the dung of pigs.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. Background to Sutri: how reformers looked to the emperor to drive simony out of Italy. If the letter is to Henry II, it predates Sutri by a generation; either way it is about simony, not about judging a pope.
