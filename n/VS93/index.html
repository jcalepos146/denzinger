John Paul II · 1978–2005

VS 93 Martyrdom, the exaltation of the inviolable holiness of God's law

[From the Encyclical Letter, "Veritatis Splendor," Aug. 6, 1993]

Papal teachingEditorial grade · Authoritative teaching

... Although martyrdom represents the high point of the witness to moral truth, and one to which relatively few people are called, there is nonetheless a consistent witness which all Christians must daily be ready to make, even at the cost of suffering and grave sacrifice. Indeed, faced with the many difficulties which fidelity to the moral order can demand, even in the most ordinary circumstances, the Christian is called, with the grace of God invoked in prayer, to a sometimes heroic commitment. ...

Editorial assessment · Authoritative teaching (VS 22–VS 120). The foundations of moral theology: freedom and law, conscience, the fundamental option, and intrinsically evil acts which no intention or circumstance can make good (VS 80). Many theologians hold the teaching on intrinsically evil acts to be definitive; the encyclical presents it as the constant teaching of the Church.
