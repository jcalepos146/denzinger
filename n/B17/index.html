Council of Basel and its aftermath · 1431–1463 · Vienna, December 1452

Supplement B17 Aeneas Silvius Piccolomini, legate of Nicholas V, on Constance and papal deposition

[Oration "Sentio," delivered at Vienna as papal legate, against an appeal from the pope to a future council. Edited and translated by Michael von Cotta-Schønberg, Orations of Pope Pius II, 20 (2019; final version 2025), p. 178; Latin in L. A. Muratori, Anecdota II, p. 121, quoted by Mansi in Natalis Alexander, Historia ecclesiastica, ed. Roncaglia and Mansi, vol. 9 (Venice, 1776), p. 364. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

But however it may be, it is clear that only in this one case is a certain and undoubted pope subject to a council: if somebody occupying the papacy falls into heresy, he immediately ceases to be pope and becomes less than any Christian. And if he is a schismatic, he cannot be called a pope, for either he suffers the same affliction or his right to the papacy is in doubt. As implied in the decree of Konstanz [Haec Sancta Synodus], a certain and undoubted pope may never be deposed by a council unless he has scandalised the universal Church, something that would be seen when many complained about him from various parts of the world. This could happen if the pope gave wives back to the priests, committed a blood crime, or introduced a new rite that displeased the community of the faithful. But nothing of this sort is happening now. So, in the present case, a council is not above the pope, and there is no [legitimate] cause for an appeal to it.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. How the papal side read Haec Sancta after Basel, from the man who as Pius II forbade appeals to a council in 1460 (n. 717) and in 1463 retracted his own Basel-era conciliarism in In minoribus agentes (B18). He limits the decree to a pope who is not certain or has ceased to be pope: the old canonists' exception for heresy (Gratian, D. 40 c. 6, "unless he is found to have strayed from the faith"), widened to grave scandal to the whole Church. That second case is his own reading; later teaching holds that the First See is judged by no one (Vatican I, n. 1830; canon 1404 of the 1983 Code).
