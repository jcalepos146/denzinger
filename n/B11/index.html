Council of Basel and its aftermath · 1431–1463 · Florence, 4 September 1439

Supplement B11 Moyses vir Dei: Florence condemns Basel

[Decree of Eugene IV with the approval of the Council of Florence, 4 September 1439. Translated for this site from the Latin as given on vatican.va, which follows Conciliorum Oecumenicorum Decreta (Bologna, 2013); the closing clauses are abridged there as here. Denzinger–Hünermann (43rd ed.) prints an excerpt. Compare a critical edition before citing.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Definitive

Eugene, bishop, servant of the servants of God, for an everlasting record.

Moses, the man of God, zealous for the salvation of the people entrusted to him, and fearing that because of the seditious schism of Korah, Dathan and Abiram the wrath of God would rise against that people if it followed them, said to the whole people at the Lord's command: "Depart from the tents of the wicked men, and touch nothing of theirs, lest you be swept away in their sins" [Num. 16:26]. For he had perceived, by the Lord's own inspiration, that those seditious men and schismatics would meet the gravest punishment, as the event afterwards showed, when the earth itself could not bear them, but by the just judgment of God swallowed them, so that they went down alive into hell.

So we too, to whom, unskilled though we are, the Lord Jesus Christ has deigned to entrust his people, hearing of that execrable crime which certain lost men staying at Basel have lately plotted in order to split the unity of the holy Church, and fearing that they may lead the unwary astray by their deceits and infect them with their poisons, are compelled to cry out with a like voice to the people of our Lord Jesus Christ entrusted to us: "Depart from the tents of the wicked men"; the more so since the Christian people is far greater than the people of the Jews was then, the Church holier than the synagogue, and the vicar of Christ superior in authority and dignity to Moses himself.

We began long ago to foresee this impiety of the men of Basel, since we saw that what was then the council of Basel was already sliding into tyranny. Many, even of lower rank, were made to come to it and to stay there at the will of the faction's authors; the votes and judgments of some were extorted by various arts, and others were led astray by lies and frauds; nearly everything gave way to conspiracies, plots, monopolies and conventicles, and a long prolonging of that council was sought out of ambition for the papacy. There, finally, innumerable novelties, disorders, deformities and almost endless evils were committed, and to it flocked clerics not yet in sacred orders, ignorant, inexperienced, vagrant and unruly men, fugitives, apostates, men condemned for crimes and escaped from prison, rebels against us and against their own superiors, and other monsters of men of that kind, who drew every stain of corruption from those very masters of crime.

We also gave our attention to that most holy work of the union of the Eastern Church, which we saw to be in grave danger through the fraud of certain factious men. Wishing therefore to provide against such great evils as far as lay in us, for the reasons aforesaid and for other reasonable and necessary causes, set out in full in the decree of transfer, we transferred what was then the council of Basel to the city of Ferrara, on the advice of our venerable brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman Church, and with the approval of very many venerable brothers and beloved sons, archbishops, bishops, bishops-elect, abbots and other prelates of churches, and masters and doctors; and there, by the Lord's disposing, we founded an ecumenical council with the Western and the Eastern Church.

Then, when plague came and continued, we transferred it, by God's favour and with the approval of the same sacred council, to this city of Florence. In it the most loving and merciful God has shown his wonders. That most pernicious schism, which had lasted in God's Church for almost five hundred years with the greatest harm to all Christendom, and to root out which very many holy Roman pontiffs our predecessors, and many kings, princes and other Christians, once laboured hard at great toil and expense, has been removed, after many public and private disputations in both places, various treatises and manifold labours; and the most holy union of Latins and Greeks has been happily accomplished, as is set out more fully in the decree made on it and solemnly promulgated.

For this we gave unfailing thanks to the eternal God, and rejoicing with all the faithful people we offered him a sacrifice of jubilation and praise. For we saw not one nation, like the Hebrew people, called to the land of promise, but the peoples, nations and tongues of many nations running together to the one word and reward of divine truth. By this, great hope is now also given that the sun of justice [Mal. 4:2], rising in the east, may spread the rays of its light into the darkness of many other nations, even unbelievers, and that the salvation of the Lord may reach to the ends of the earth [Isa. 49:6].

Of this we already hold, by God's disposing, the best pledges. In these days almighty God has granted, through our efforts, that envoys of the Armenians, with full powers, should come to us, to the Apostolic See and to this sacred council from the farthest parts of the north (the Armenian envoys reached Florence on 13 August 1439). Looking on us no otherwise than on blessed Peter, prince of the apostles, and venerating us, and acknowledging this holy see as mother and teacher of all the faithful, they professed that they had come to it and to the said council for spiritual food and the truth of sound doctrine. For this we again gave many thanks to our God.

But what troubles, what attacks, what persecutions we have suffered until now in this divine work, not from Turks or Saracens, but from men called Christians, the mind shudders to recall.

Blessed Jerome relates (Letter to Paulinus) that from the time of Hadrian to the reign of Constantine an image of Jupiter was worshipped in the place of the Lord's resurrection, and on the rock of the cross a marble statue of Venus set up by the pagans, the authors of the persecution thinking that they would take from us faith in the resurrection and the cross if they polluted the holy places with idols.

Something not unlike this has befallen us and the Church of God in these days from those most abandoned men of Basel, except that that was done by pagans who did not know the true God, and this by men who have known and hated [John 15:24]. So their pride, as the prophet says, rises up continually [Ps. 73:23], and the more dangerously in this, that they spread their poisons under the appearance of reform, which in themselves they have always abhorred.

First, after those who were the authors of all the scandal at Basel had failed the Greeks in what they had promised, they learned plainly from the envoys of the Greeks and of the Eastern Church that our dearest son in Christ John Palaeologus, illustrious emperor of the Romans, and Joseph of good memory, patriarch of Constantinople, and the rest, both prelates and others of the Eastern Church, wished to come to the place lawfully chosen for celebrating an ecumenical council by our legates and presidents and by others of the more eminent there, to whom the right of choosing the place had passed under an agreement confirmed by the common consent of that council after grave dissensions among its members. They learned too that we, at the petition and urgent request of those envoys, had confirmed this choice of place in a general consistory at Bologna, and were sending galleys and other things needed for this most holy work of union toward Constantinople at great labour and expense. Then they dared to decree against us and the said cardinals a detestable monitory or citation (approved at Basel on 25 January 1438), to cut off this holy work, and to send it to the said emperor and patriarch at Constantinople so as to turn them altogether from coming. For they knew that they would by no means come to any place other than the one chosen, as was said above.

Next, when they learned that the said emperor and patriarch and the rest were already coming to us for this work of most holy union, they tried to throw another snare of impiety over so divine a thing, by pronouncing against us a sacrilegious sentence, as they call it, of suspension from the administration of the papacy.

Lastly, the said leaders of the scandals, very few in number, most of them of the lowest condition and name, truly the greatest haters of peace, adding iniquity to iniquity so as not to enter into the justice of the Lord [Ps. 68:28], when they saw the grace of the Holy Spirit already working in us for the union of the Greeks, swerved from the straight line through the windings of error and held a pretended session on the seventeenth day before the kalends of June last (16 May 1439). Claiming to adhere to certain decrees issued at Constance by only one of the three obediences, after the departure of John XXIII, as he was called in that obedience, while the schism still raged, they put forward three propositions, which they call truths of faith, as if to make heretics of us and of all princes, prelates and other faithful and devoted sons of the Apostolic See. Their tenor follows in these words:

"The truth of the power of a general council representing the universal Church over the pope and anyone else, declared by the general councils of Constance and of this Basel, is a truth of the Catholic faith.

"The truth that the pope can by no means, by his authority, dissolve a general council representing the universal Church, lawfully assembled in act, concerning the matters declared in the aforesaid truth or any of them, nor prorogue it to another time, nor transfer it from place to place, without its consent, is a truth of the Catholic faith.

"Whoever obstinately resists the aforesaid truths is to be judged a heretic." (Decree of Basel, session 33.)

In this these most pernicious men, while they colour their malice with the paint of a truth of faith, drag the council of Constance into an evil and reprobate sense wholly foreign to its teaching, following the doctrine of other schismatics and heretics, who always strive to build up for themselves invented errors and impious dogmas from the divine Scriptures and the holy Fathers perversely understood.

Finally, turning their mind away altogether and bending down their eyes so as not to see heaven nor remember just judgments [Dan. 13:9], after the manner of Dioscorus and the condemned synod of Ephesus, they proceeded with an inexpiable crime to a poisoned and execrable sentence, as they assert, of declaration or deprivation (decree of Basel, session 34) from the dignity and office of the supreme apostolate. We wish its tenor, horrible to every pious mind, to be held as sufficiently expressed here. They have left nothing undone, as far as in them lay, to overturn entirely this incomparable good of union.

O wretched and degenerate sons! O wicked and adulterous generation [Matt. 12:39]! What more cruel than this impiety and iniquity could be devised? What more detestable, more horrible, more insane? They had once said that nothing better, more glorious or more fruitful than this most holy union had been seen or heard among the Christian people since the birth of the Church; that it was not fitting to quarrel over the place in such a matter, but that to obtain it not only the goods of this world but body and soul should be risked. They cried this to the whole world and stirred up the Christian people to it, as is set out fully in their decrees and letters. Now they persecute it so furiously and so impiously, as far as in them lies, that the demons of the whole world seem to have flocked together to that robber council of Basel.

Almighty God has not so far allowed their iniquity, which has always lied to itself [Ps. 26:12], to prevail. Yet they strive with all their strength to complete it, even to the abomination of desolation in the Church of God [Matt. 24:15]; and we can in no way pass over such things without the gravest offence to God and imminent danger of confusion and abomination in the Church of God. Therefore, in keeping with the duty of our pastoral office, and urged to it by many who burn with zeal for God, wishing to meet such great evils, to provide fittingly and wholesomely as far as in us lies, and to cast this execrable impiety and most pernicious plague out of the Church of God, we follow in the footsteps of our predecessors, who, as Pope Nicholas of holy memory writes, were accustomed to annul councils wickedly held, even councils of all the bishops, as happened with the universal second synod of Ephesus, which the most blessed Pope Leo set aside, and instituted Chalcedon. With the approval of this sacred council of Florence we renew the solemn and salutary decree of declaration against those sacrilegious men, issued by us by apostolic authority in the sacred general council of Ferrara on the fifteenth day before the kalends of March (15 February 1438).

By it, among other things, we declared, with the approval of that sacred council of Ferrara, that each and all who were at Basel under the name of a pretended council, which we said is more truly a conventicle, acting against our transfer and declaration and presuming those scandalous and wicked things, even if they shone with the dignity of cardinal, patriarch, archbishop, bishop, abbot or any other ecclesiastical or secular dignity, had incurred the penalties contained in our said letters of transfer: excommunication, deprivation of dignities, benefices and offices, and incapacity for the future.

Now also, with the approval of this sacred council, we decree and declare that whatever was done and attempted by the said impious men staying at Basel, of which mention is made in our said decree of Ferrara, and likewise each and all things done, performed and attempted by them afterwards, especially in the two pretended sessions, or rather conspiracies, last named, and whatever may have followed or may in future follow from them, as done by impious men having no power, but cast out and rejected by God, were and are null, void and of no force, as presumed in fact only and wholly without effect, strength or weight. And the propositions written above, according to the perverse understanding of the men of Basel, which they show by their deeds, as contrary to the sound sense of sacred Scripture, of the holy Fathers and of the council of Constance itself; and also the said alleged sentence of declaration or deprivation, with all that has followed from it and may follow in the future, as impious and scandalous, and tending to the manifest rending of the Church of God and to the confusion of all ecclesiastical order and Christian rule, we condemn and reject, with the approval of this sacred council, and declare them condemned and rejected.

We also decree and declare that each and all of the aforesaid were and are schismatics and heretics, and that, besides the penalties declared in the said council of Ferrara, they are to be punished with fitting penalties as such, together with all their supporters and defenders, of whatever state, condition and ecclesiastical or secular rank, even if they shine with the dignity of cardinal, patriarch, archbishop, bishop, abbot or any other dignity, so that they receive their deserved portion with the said Korah, Dathan and Abiram [Num. 16]. Let no one therefore … If anyone, however …

Editorial assessment · Definitive. Eugene IV's decree, with the approval of the Council of Florence. It condemns Basel's three propositions "according to the perverse understanding" of Basel, shown by its deeds, as contrary to the sound sense of Scripture, the Fathers "and the council of Constance itself," and declares their authors schismatics and heretics. It condemns Basel's reading of Constance, not Constance, and notes that the decrees Basel relied on were issued at Constance by one of three obediences while the schism lasted. Vatican I excludes the same claim (n. 1830).
