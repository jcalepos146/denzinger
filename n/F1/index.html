Council of Florence: decrees not in Denzinger · 1439–1445 · Session 13, Rome, 30 November 1444

Supplement F1 Multa et admirabilia: the bull of union with the Syrians

[Bull of Eugene IV with the approval of the council, then sitting at the Lateran. Translated for this site from the Latin of Conciliorum Oecumenicorum Decreta as given by Documenta Catholica Omnia; the closing clauses are abridged there as here. Compare a critical edition before citing.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Defined dogma

Eugene, bishop, servant of the servants of God, for an everlasting record.

Many and wonderful gifts, far greater than we could ourselves have desired or imagined, does the unspeakable mercy of God's compassion bestow on his holy Church in our days. So we see the orthodox faith spreading, new peoples returning day by day to the obedience of the Apostolic See, and the causes of joy and exultation for us and for all Christ's faithful renewed daily, so that we are rightly moved again and again to say with the prophet in jubilation to the faithful peoples: "Come, let us exult in the Lord, let us sing joyfully to God our saviour, for great is the Lord and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, on his holy mountain" [Ps. 94:1; 47:2].

In the Catholic Church, which is the city of God founded on the holy mountain of the Apostolic See and on the authority of Peter, God, whose omnipotence and wisdom are without number, has always worked great and unsearchable things. Yet the unspeakable providence of the Creator has granted it this singular and chief gift: that the orthodox faith, which alone gives life to the human race and alone sanctifies it, should always remain on that holy mountain in one unchanging confession of the truth; and that the dissensions which arise against the Church from the variety of earthly opinions, and which separate men from the solidity of that rock, should be rooted out and destroyed when they return to that mountain. So it comes about that the peoples and nations flowing into its bosom come together with it in the one truth of faith.

These gifts of God, so great, so lofty, so unusual, the immensity of his goodness has let us behold, not by our merits but by his kindness and favour alone. After the union in the sacred ecumenical council of Florence of the Greeks, who seemed to differ from the Roman Church in certain articles, and after the return of the Armenians and the Jacobites, who were entangled in various opinions and at last, leaving all dissension, came together in the one right path of truth,

see, now again, with the Lord's help, other nations have come from afar, dwelling in Mesopotamia between the Tigris and the Euphrates, who did not think rightly about the procession of the Holy Spirit and certain other articles.

Great then is the cause of rejoicing for us and for all Christ's faithful. For now, by the Lord's favour, the most splendid assertion of the Roman Church concerning the truth of faith, which has always been clean of every stain of falsehood, shines with new flashes even in the East beyond the borders of the Euphrates. It has drawn and called our venerable brother Abdala, archbishop of Edessa, legate of our venerable brother Ignatius, patriarch of the Syrians, and of his whole nation, to this kindly city, into our presence and to this sacred ecumenical council of the Lateran, humbly and devoutly asking that we hand on to them the rule of faith which the holy Roman Church professes.

Among all the cares of the holy Apostolic See we hold this as our first and chief care, as we always have: to defend the truth of the Gospel and, heresies being rooted out, to spread and propagate the orthodox faith far and wide. We therefore chose some of our venerable brothers, cardinals of the holy Roman Church, who, calling in some masters of sacred Scripture from this sacred council, were to confer with the said archbishop about the difficulties, doubts and errors of that nation, to examine him, to open to him the teaching of Catholic truth, and finally to instruct and teach him fully about the integrity of the faith of the Roman Church.

They found that he thought rightly in all matters of faith and morals, except only three articles: the procession of the Holy Spirit, and the two natures in Christ Jesus our saviour and the same number of wills and operations in him. On these they set out and opened to him the truth of the orthodox faith, offered him the understanding of the sacred Scriptures and the testimonies of the holy doctors, and brought forward the truest reasons suited to the matter.

Having understood their teaching, the archbishop declared that all his doubts had been fully satisfied. He professed such views both on the procession of the Holy Spirit and on the two natures and two wills and operations in Christ Jesus our Lord that he seemed to understand the truth of faith fully; and he professed that, in the name of the said patriarch, of the whole nation and in his own, he would accept all the faith and all the teaching which we should hand on to him with the approval of this sacred council.

Filled with all joy in Christ at this, we poured out immense thanks to our God, because we see our wish fulfilled for the salvation of that nation.

Therefore, after careful discussion with our brothers and with the sacred council, we decreed, with the approval of the same sacred council, to hand on and assign to the said archbishop, receiving it in the names given above, the faith and teaching which the holy Roman mother Church holds.

This, then, is the faith which the holy Roman mother Church has always held, preached and taught, and which she now holds, preaches, professes and teaches. We decree that the said archbishop Abdala, on behalf and in the name of the said patriarch of the Syrians and of that whole nation, and in his own, shall accept it on these three articles, and guard and observe it for all time to come.

First, that the Holy Spirit is eternally from the Father and the Son, and has his essence and his subsistent being from the Father together with the Son, and proceeds from both eternally as from one principle and by one spiration.

Further, it holds, professes and teaches that one and the same Son of God and of man, our Lord Jesus Christ, is perfect in divinity and perfect in humanity, true God and true man, of a rational soul and a body, consubstantial with the Father according to his divinity and consubstantial with us according to his humanity, like us in all things except sin; begotten of the Father before the ages, and in the last days the same, for us and for our salvation, born of the Virgin Mary, Mother of God, according to his humanity; one and the same Christ, true only-begotten Son of God, to be acknowledged in two natures without confusion, without change, without division, without separation, the difference of the natures being by no means removed by the union, but rather the property of each nature being preserved and concurring in one person and one subsistence; not parted or divided into two persons, but one and the same Son of God and of man, our Lord Jesus Christ.

It believes, professes and teaches, further, that in the one Lord Jesus Christ there are two natural operations, without division, without change, without separation, without confusion, according to the teaching of the holy Fathers; and also two natural wills, one divine and the other human, not contrary to each other, but his human will subject to his divine and almighty will.

And as his most holy animated flesh was deified and not destroyed, but remained in its own state and nature, so his human will was deified and not destroyed, but rather saved and perfected.

We decree that the same archbishop Abdala, in the names given above, must accept this faith, hold it in his heart and profess it with his mouth.

We further ordain and decree that, in the same names, he must receive and embrace whatever the holy Roman Church has defined and ordained at various times, especially the decrees for the Greeks, the Armenians and the Jacobites issued in the sacred ecumenical council of Florence. These, translated into Arabic, the archbishop Abdala read carefully and praised, and we had them handed to him, receiving them in the names given above, for the fuller instruction of all. He must also, in the same names, approve and accept whatever doctors and holy Fathers the holy Roman Church approves and accepts, and hold as condemned and rejected whatever persons or anything else she condemns and rejects.

He promised, as a true son of obedience, in the names given above, on oath, always devoutly and faithfully to obey the ordinances and commands of the Apostolic See.

Let no one therefore … If anyone, however …

Editorial assessment · Defined dogma. Eugene IV, with the approval of the council in its Lateran sessions, hands on to the Syrians the faith on the three articles in question. Each restates defined doctrine: the procession of the Holy Spirit as in the decree for the Greeks (n. 691), the two natures as at Chalcedon (n. 148), and the two wills and operations as at Constantinople III (n. 290–291). It also binds them to the decrees for the Greeks, Armenians and Jacobites.
