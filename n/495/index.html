John XXII · 1316–1334

n. 495 The Poverty of Christ

[From the edict "Cum inter nonnullos," Nov. 13, 1323]

DS 941Papal teachingEditorial grade · Condemned as heresy

(1) That what we read about Christ in the Gospel of St. Matthew, that He Himself paid tribute to Caesar, when He ordered the stater which had been taken from the mouth of the fish [cf.Matt. 17:26] to be given to those who sought a drachma, He did this not with condescension out of liberality or piety, but forced by necessity.

[ Thence according to the Bull they concluded ]:

That all temporal affairs of the Church are subject to the emperor and he can accept these things as his own.

Latin text · DS 941

941(1) Quod illud, quod de Christo legitur in Evangelio beati Matthaei (17, 26), quod ipse solvit tributum Caesari, quando staterem sumptum ex ore piscis illis qui petebant didrachma iussit dari (dare), hoc facit non condescensive e liberalitate sive pietate (suae pietatis), sed necessitate coactus. (Inde in bulla ex mente Marsilii concluditur:) Quod omnia temporalia Ecclesiae subsunt Imperatori, et ea potest accipere velut sua.

Editorial assessment · Condemned as heresy (n. 494–500). John XXII's Cum inter nonnullos (1323): the assertion that Christ and the apostles owned nothing at all is declared heretical.
