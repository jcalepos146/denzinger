Leo XIV · 2025–

VAM 73 Chapter IV The Centrality of the Kerygma

[From the Note of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith on the transmission of the faith in an epochal change, "Venite ad Me," Oct. 22, 2026]

Doctrine of the FaithEditorial grade · Authoritative teaching

Divine revelation, as the Second Vatican Council teaches, is not merely the transmission of doctrinal truths or an “instruction” from God to humanity, but a covenant dialogue: “God, who is invisible, out of the abundance of his love, speaks to human beings as friends and associates with them, to invite them and admit them into fellowship with himself.” This means that the kerygma —the fundamental proclamation of the faith—requires a response from those who receive it that is not limited to the intellectual level but involves their entire existence in accepting God’s call to share in his very life. Precisely because it is not confined to the realm of knowledge alone, but has, so to speak, an existential reach (cf. Jn 17:3), such a response is, by its very nature, an event of salvation: “If you confess with your lips that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved” ( Rom 10:9).
