Gregory IX · 1227–1241

n. 446 The Invalidity of Marriage Subject to Conditions

[From fragments of the Decrees n. 104, about the years 1227-1234]

DS 827Papal teachingEditorial grade · Authoritative teaching

If conditions contrary to the nature of marriage are inserted, for example, if one says to the other: "I contract marriage with you, if you avoid the generation of children," or "until I find another more worthy by reason of reputation or riches," or, "if you surrender yourself to adultery for money," the marriage contract, however favorable it may be, is lacking in effect; although some conditions appended in matrimony, if they are disgraceful or impossible, because of its esteem, are to be considered as not added.

Latin text · DS 827

827Si condiciones contra substantiam coniugii inserantur, puta, si alter dicat alteri: 'contraho tecum, si generationem prolis evites', vel: 'donec inveniam aliam honore vel facultatibus digniorem', aut: 'si pro quaestu adulterandam te tradas': matrimonialis contractus, quantumcumque sit favorabilis, caret effectu; licet aliae condiciones appositae in matrimonio, si turpes aut impossibiles fuerint, debeant propter eius favorem pro non adiectis haberi. 1.289 1.290
