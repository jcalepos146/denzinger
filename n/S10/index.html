The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Lenten synod, Rome, 7 March 1080

Supplement S10 St. Gregory VII: "I accompanied the lord Pope Gregory beyond the mountains"

[Pope St. Gregory VII, second sentence of excommunication and deposition of Henry IV (Register VII, 14a); English from E. Emerton, The Correspondence of Pope Gregory VII, p. 150. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

For you know that I entered holy orders not of my own pleasure, and that I accompanied the lord Pope Gregory [VI] unwillingly beyond the mountains, but still more unwillingly returned with my master Pope [St.] Leo [IX] to your special church, where I have served you as best I could; and then most unwillingly and unworthy as I was, to my great grief and with groans and lamentations I was set upon your throne. I say this because it is not I that have chosen you, but you that have chosen me and laid upon me the heavy burden of your Church.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. Hildebrand went into exile with Gregory VI and, as pope, called him "the lord Pope Gregory"; he took the name Gregory in 1073. The Annuario Pontificio counts Gregory VI as a legitimate pope.
