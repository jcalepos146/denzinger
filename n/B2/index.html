Council of Basel and its aftermath · 1431–1463 · 15 December 1433

Supplement B2 Eugene IV, Dudum sacrum: the council recognized

[Summary written for this site from Dudum sacrum, in Mansi 29, cols. 78–79, and the acts of Basel session 16.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Weight disputed

Under pressure from the council and the princes, Eugene IV withdrew his dissolution and declared that the council had been lawfully continued from its beginning and should proceed with its purposes: the uprooting of heresy, peace among Christians, and reform.

Eugene said Deus novit had never issued from him or with his knowledge and revoked it as a precaution. Basel accepted Dudum sacrum at session 16 on 5 February 1434 and incorporated the revoked letters in its acts.

Editorial assessment · Weight disputed. Conciliarists read it as papal approval of Basel and its claims. Others note that it recognized the council's legitimacy without ratifying its decrees or accepting its supremacy, and that the pope later reserved the rights of the Apostolic See.
