The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Sutri, December 1046, in the Life of Leo IX (11th century)

Supplement S6 Bonus of Cervia: the Romans ask the emperor for a shepherd

[Bonus II, bishop of Cervia, Life and Miracles of Leo IX, in Analecta Bollandiana 25, pp. 275–276. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

From him [Benedict IX] simoniacal heresy grew, from him many homicides and adulteries grew, from him perjuries, from him thefts, from him false testimonies, and even by him the holy churches were destroyed. And when for many years the church was possessed by this iniquity, mindful of the Lord Jesus Christ and the world which He created, He sent a most victorious messenger. King Henry arose after the passing of Conon [Conrad II], his father; the Lord sent him to the parts of Italy to destroy the heresy by which it was held. Just as the only-begotten Son of God came to redeem the lost world, so He too sent the bountiful king to liberate Rome from all errors. When, however, strifes and wars arose among the Romans, among themselves fighting, they wanted to kill that most iniquitous bishop.

But it was not granted by the Lord; seeking flight into the camp which is called Monte Gabum, he was liberated. Meanwhile, the most gracious Emperor Henry came to the city of Sutri and there ordered a tribunal to be prepared and all the Roman people to be gathered. When all the Romans were gathered, they began to cry out with one voice: "Most just Emperor, for the Lord has established you to protect the people of the Romans; and we beseech your piety that you grant us a shepherd" […]. Then the emperor, having called everyone to himself, said: "Choose for yourselves whomever you wish, and I will choose according to your will."

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. Shows the role the Romans gave the emperor at Sutri: protector who grants them a shepherd at their request. Written in praise of the reform, after the fact.
