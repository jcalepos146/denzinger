Council of Constance: decrees not in Denzinger · 1414–1418 · Session 5 (6 April 1415)

Supplement C1 Haec Sancta: the authority of the council

[Translated for this site from the Latin text of the decree. A shorter form had been read at the fourth session; this is the form adopted at the fifth. Compare a critical edition before citing.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Weight disputed

This holy synod of Constance, forming a general council for the ending of the present schism and for the union and reform of the Church of God in head and members, lawfully assembled in the Holy Spirit to the praise of almighty God, in order that the union and reform of the Church of God may be obtained more easily, more securely, more fully and more freely, ordains, defines, enacts, decrees and declares as follows.

First, that this synod, lawfully assembled in the Holy Spirit, forming a general council and representing the Catholic Church militant, has its power immediately from Christ; and that everyone, of whatever state or dignity, even if it be papal, is bound to obey it in what pertains to the faith, to the ending of the said schism, and to the general reform of the said Church of God in head and members.

Next, it declares that anyone, of whatever condition, state or dignity, even if it be papal, who contumaciously refuses to obey the mandates, statutes, ordinances or precepts of this holy synod, or of any other general council lawfully assembled, on the matters named above or on what pertains to them, whether already made or to be made, shall, unless he repents, be subjected to fitting penance and duly punished, recourse being had, if necessary, to other aids of the law.

Editorial assessment · Weight disputed. Adopted while three men claimed the papacy and the claimant who had convoked the council, John XXIII, had fled. No pope confirmed it as such: Martin V approved what the council decided conciliariter in matters of faith, and Eugene IV accepted Constance only without prejudice to the rights, dignity and pre-eminence of the Apostolic See. Vatican I's definitions on the primacy exclude the conciliarist reading. Whether it was a dogmatic claim, an emergency measure for a schism, or a legal decree is still argued.

Source and coverage: Haec Sancta: the authority of the council · unreviewed locally. Passage grades are editorial assessments.
