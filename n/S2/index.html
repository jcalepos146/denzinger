The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Liber ad amicum (1085–1086) and Dialogi (c. 1076–1079)

Supplement S2 Bonizo of Sutri and Desiderius of Monte Cassino: Gregory VI deposes himself

[Bonizo of Sutri, Liber ad amicum, book 5, in MGH Libelli de lite I, ed. E. Dümmler (1891), pp. 571–620; Desiderius of Monte Cassino (later Pope Victor III), Dialogi, book 3, in PL 149. Summary written for this site from the account in P. Schaff, History of the Christian Church IV, ch. 4, notes 301–302; the Latin has not been checked.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

Gregory VI presided at the opening of the synod, which he had convoked at the emperor's wish. Sylvester III was condemned as an intruder; Benedict IX, who had resigned, did not appear and was deposed a few days later at Rome. Gregory, asked how he had obtained the papacy, acknowledged the payment made to Benedict.

On the account of these two Gregorian writers, the synod did not pass sentence on him. Since he had acted in good faith and was otherwise worthy, he was allowed to judge his own case: he pronounced his own deposition, the fathers answered that his pleasure was theirs, and he asked pardon and laid down the pontifical office. Other sources, among them Peter Damian (S7) and the Annales Corbeienses, say that the synod deposed him.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. The self-deposition account, from two writers of the Gregorian party. If it is right, the synod honoured the axiom that the first see is judged by no one: it established the facts, and the pope judged his own case. Summary only; read Bonizo and Desiderius before citing.
