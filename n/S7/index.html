The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Letter 72 (c. 1060)

Supplement S7 St. Peter Damian: the abdicating pope and the deposed one

[St. Peter Damian, Letter 72 (Opusculum 19), 11, in PL 145, col. 441; English from O. J. Blum, The Letters of Peter Damian 61–90, The Fathers of the Church, Mediaeval Continuation 3, p. 145. The bracketed names are the contributor's. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

Did not the lesser Pope Benedict [IX], to whom we referred above, give up the administration of the Apostolic See and establish Gregory, who was called Gratian, as his successor in the Roman Church? Later, when the council had debated these events with the emperor Henry present, since payment had intervened, he [Gregory VI] who accepted the money was deposed, and he [Benedict IX] who abdicated was not excommunicated.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. Peter Damian says the council deposed Gregory VI because money had passed, and that Benedict IX, who had abdicated, was not excommunicated. Benedict received the payment and Gregory made it, so "he who accepted the money" probably means the one who received the papacy; check the Latin in PL 145.
