The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Anonymous tract (c. 1047–1048)

Supplement S9 De ordinando pontifice: a simoniac is no pope, and his case belongs to the bishops, not the emperor

[Anonymous, De ordinando pontifice, in MGH Libelli de lite I, p. 10 (first paragraph) and p. 14 (second). The translation of the second passage is tentative. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

This man [Gregory VI], however, bought the gift of the Holy Spirit, and what he bought is nothing. We have said that sin [simony] is evil, and so the evil man is nothing.

But if, again, it is a question whether he is not to be discussed, we reply: not his [the emperor's], but the bishops' concern. The confession of that man does not pertain to the emperor, although in the council, while he acted well, he is not to be refuted. Where then are the emperors of Christ found to obtain a place? If it were truer for us to say, rather that they rise up to the office of the devil, in sword and blood, so that, while they are made penitent by penance, the vices of the spirit are cut away, they themselves, being mad, either in the cutting off of the cloak or in the amputation of the limbs of the flesh; which by grace before God is altogether abominable.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. A second contemporary objection from another angle: simony made Gregory VI's election null, so there was no pope to depose; and whatever judgment was needed belonged to the bishops, not the emperor.
