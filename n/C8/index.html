Council of Constance: decrees not in Denzinger · 1414–1418 · Session 42 (28 December 1417)

Supplement C8 Martin V releases Baldassare Cossa, formerly John XXIII

[Bull of Martin V, read in session 42. Latin in Mansi 27, col. 1172; Hardt, Magnum Oecumenicum Constantiense Concilium IV (1699), col. 1500; Monumenta Conciliorum Generalium III/1, 1185. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the scan.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Juridical act

Latin opening: "Dudum præsens sacrum generale Concilium Constantiense personam Baltassaris Cossa, olim Joannis Papæ vigesimitertii in sua obedientia nuncupati, post illius a papatu per definitivam sententiam ejusdem Concilii canonicam depositionem per eamdem sententiam…"

Formerly the present holy general Council of Constance condemned the person of Balthasar Cossa, formerly called Pope John XXIII in his obedience, after his canonical deposition from the papacy by the definitive sentence of the same Council, by that same sentence, to stand and to remain in some good and honest place, under custody and safekeeping in the name of the said holy Council, for as long as it should seem good to the same holy Council for the good of the union of the Church of God: whom hitherto you have faithfully and diligently preserved, both through you and through our beloved son, the noble man Louis, Duke of Bavaria and Count Palatine of the Rhine, in your name, under trust and safe custody, honorably and duly, as a zealot and devoted servant of union and peace…

The bull provides for his release from that custody. Cossa was freed in 1419, submitted to Martin V at Florence, was made cardinal bishop of Tusculum, and died that December.

Editorial assessment · Juridical act. A juridical act of Martin V, promulgated in the council after his election. In his own words the deposition of session 12 (C2) was "canonical" and "from the papacy" by "definitive sentence." It shows how Martin regarded the council's acts against the claimants; by itself it says nothing about Haec Sancta (C1).
