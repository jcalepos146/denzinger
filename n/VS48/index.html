John Paul II · 1978–2005

VS 48 Jesus Christ, the true light that enlightens everyone

[From the Encyclical Letter, "Veritatis Splendor," Aug. 6, 1993]

Papal teachingEditorial grade · Authoritative teaching

... It contradicts the Church's teachings on the unity of the human person, whose rational soul is per se et essentialiter the form of his body. The spiritual and immortal soul is the principle of unity of the human being, whereby it exists as a whole — corpore et anima unus — as a person. These definitions not only point out that the body, which has been promised the resurrection, will also share in glory. They also remind us that reason and free will are linked with all the bodily and sense faculties. The person, including the body, is completely entrusted to himself, and it is in the unity of body and soul that the person is the subject of his own moral acts. The person, by the light of reason and the support of virtue, discovers in the body the anticipatory signs, the expression and the promise of the gift of self, in conformity with the wise plan of the Creator. ... And since the human person cannot be reduced to a freedom which is self-designing, but entails a particular spiritual and bodily structure, the primordial moral requirement of loving and respecting the person as an end and never as a mere means also implies, by its very nature, respect for certain fundamental goods, without which one would fall into relativism and arbitrariness.

Editorial assessment · Authoritative teaching (VS 22–VS 120). The foundations of moral theology: freedom and law, conscience, the fundamental option, and intrinsically evil acts which no intention or circumstance can make good (VS 80). Many theologians hold the teaching on intrinsically evil acts to be definitive; the encyclical presents it as the constant teaching of the Church.
