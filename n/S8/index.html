The Synod of Sutri and the deposition of Gregory VI · 1046–1080 · Letter to Henry III after the death of Clement II (late 1047)

Supplement S8 Wazo of Liège: the deposition was unlawful, and the see is reserved to Gregory VI

[Wazo, bishop of Liège, as quoted by Anselm of Liège, Gesta episcoporum Tungrensium, Traiectensium et Leodiensium, in MGH SS 7, pp. 228–229. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the edition.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Historical witness

Let your Serenity consider whether the see of the supreme pontiff, when occupants have been deposed by those who ought not to have done so, may not have been reserved by God himself. The man whom you ordered to be ordained in his stead is dead [Clement II] and seems to have yielded to the other, who is still alive [Gregory VI]. Since it has pleased you to ask our opinion on these matters, let your Highness cease to wish to put anyone in the place of him who survives. Neither divine nor human laws are certain to allow this, since the sayings and writings of the Holy Fathers everywhere attest that the supreme pontiff ought to be judged by no one but God alone. I call God to witness that, though as an unworthy priest I may incur your anger and dread in this matter, nothing truer or more excellent than this opinion can be thought of or found by me.

Editorial assessment · Historical witness. The principal contemporary objection. Wazo holds that the pope is judged by God alone, so the deposition was unlawful and the see remains Gregory VI's while he lives. Most Catholic historians reject this, since it would make Clement II, Benedict IX's third term and Damasus II invalid; it still shows that Sutri was understood as deposing Gregory.
