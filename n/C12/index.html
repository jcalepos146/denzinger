Council of Constance: decrees not in Denzinger · 1414–1418 · 13 December 1425

Supplement C12 Martin V to Władysław II of Poland: no appeal from the Roman Bishop

[Archiv für österreichische Geschichte 45 (1871), 480. Translation and citation contributed to this site; not yet checked against the scan.]

Not in DenzingerEditorial grade · Authoritative teaching

It has also found our pleasure, and brings it to expression, that the appeal to the next council lodged by your spokesmen at the Council of Constance has been withdrawn and rejected by your Highness after sound and mature deliberation, whereby you have shown your reverence towards us and your royal wisdom. For since satisfaction was sufficiently done to your honour by us, you have desisted from the unseemly appeal and have pronounced most rightly that there is no authority on earth whatsoever to which one might appeal from the Roman Bishop.

Editorial assessment · Authoritative teaching. The appeal was the Polish appeal to a future council in the Falkenberg affair (see C9). Martin V had rejected such appeals in consistory on 10 March 1418; here he praises the king for holding that there is no appeal from the pope, the principle later fixed by Pius II (n. 717).
