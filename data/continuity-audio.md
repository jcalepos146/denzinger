# Hermeneutic of ecclesial continuity: listening scripts

This is a research layer written for this site. It applies a published method to disputed texts and records where the method succeeds, where it fails and where the evidence is not yet in. Its verdicts, confidence levels and the proposed synthesis on Constance, Basel and Vatican I are scholarly hypotheses, not teaching of the Catholic Church and not a judgment of any office of the Holy See.

## A1. Cum occasione: what the five propositions condemn

What was taught. Innocent X assigns each of five propositions its own censure. The fifth calls it Semipelagian to say Christ died for all. Its strongest censure applies to the claim that he died for the salvation of the predestined alone.

By what authority. Alexander VII confirms the censures as given and declares that they were condemned in Jansenius’s intended sense. Paul V had closed the De auxiliis proceedings while forbidding the parties to censure one another.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. Christ died for all, and Trent distinguishes his universal death from the communication of its merits. Trent also affirms that prevenient grace comes first and that the will can freely cooperate or refuse.

## A2. Ad sacram: the binding force of a dogmatic fact

What was taught. Alexander VII declared that the five condemned propositions really are in Jansen's book and were condemned in the sense he meant. Clement XI added that keeping a respectful silence about this is not enough.

By what authority. Papal constitutions; a formulary of submission imposed by law. The judgment is what theologians call a dogmatic fact.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. Permanent, on the common view: that the Church can judge which books teach a condemned doctrine. Lapsed: the obligation to sign the formulary.

## A3. Unigenitus and the censure of Quesnel

What was taught. Clement the Eleventh condemned all one hundred and one propositions of Quesnel and also condemned his book.

## A5. Baius on concupiscence and the gratuity of grace

What was taught. Trent says baptism remits original sin while concupiscence remains. Pius the Fifth censured propositions of Baius on concupiscence, original integrity, and grace.

By what authority. The bull applies censures respectively. Its concession about the possible defense of some propositions is subject to a dispute about punctuation.

## A6. Quierzy and Valence on predestination and redemption

What was taught. Quierzy says Christ suffered for all and uses the image of a remedy. Valence distinguishes those who are truly redeemed from those who persevere.

By what authority. The earlier witnesses are local councils. Trent distinguishes Christ dying for all from people receiving the benefit, and Innocent the Tenth censures a specified restriction.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. The universal death and the particular reception of grace can stand together. The exact objection made by Valence still needs an explanation.

## A7. De auxiliis and the power to resist grace

What was taught. Trent teaches that grace comes first and the will can assent or dissent. Báñez and Molina offer different accounts of what makes aid efficacious.

## B1. Quanta cura and the Syllabus beside Dignitatis Humanae

What was taught. Pius IX condemned the idea that freedom of conscience and worship is everyone's right, to be written into law, with no authority able to limit it. The Second Vatican Council taught that everyone has a right not to be coerced in religion, within the limits of public order, and that this should be a civil right.

By what authority. Papal encyclicals and the Syllabus on one side; a declaration of an ecumenical council on the other. Neither is a solemn definition.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. Permanent: the duty to seek the truth, and the freedom of the act of faith. Contingent: the confessional State. Disputed: whether the two texts speak of the same right.

## B2. Lateran IV, canon 3, beside Dignitatis Humanae

What was taught. The Fourth Lateran Council told rulers to expel heretics from their lands, under Church penalties. The Second Vatican Council taught that no one may be forced in matters of religion by any human power.

By what authority. A law of an ecumenical council, long since abrogated, and a conciliar declaration. Neither is a definition.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. The law was contingent and is gone. Whether it was just when it was made is the unresolved question: this site does not claim to have answered it.

## B3. Florence, the Holy Office and salvation outside the Church

What was taught. Florence requires union with the Church before death. The Holy Office says that a person can be joined through desire, which can be implicit under certain conditions.

By what authority. The Holy Office letter was approved by Pius the Twelfth. The Second Vatican Council cites it and describes different relationships to the people of God.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. The Church is necessary for salvation. The letter names supernatural faith and charity, and the application to particular people belongs to God.

## B6. Pastor aeternus and Lumen Gentium 22: collegiality and the primacy

What was taught. Vatican I defined that the pope has full and supreme authority over the whole Church. Vatican II taught that the bishops as a body, together with the pope and never without him, also have supreme authority.

By what authority. A conciliar definition, and a dogmatic constitution with an explanatory note added by papal order.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. Permanent: both. What theologians still discuss is whether these are one subject of authority or two.

## C1. The death penalty: Innocent III, Evangelium Vitae and Catechism 2267 (2018)

What was taught. In 1210 Innocent III required converts to accept that the State may impose the death penalty without sin, if it judges justly. In 1995 John Paul II taught it should be used only in cases of absolute necessity. In 2018 the Catechism was changed to say that the death penalty is inadmissible because it attacks the dignity of the person.

By what authority. A papal profession of faith; an encyclical; a revision of the Catechism by papal rescript. None is a solemn definition.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. Permanent: the dignity of the person and the State's authority to punish justly. Contingent: what penalties are necessary now. Unresolved: whether 2018 says the penalty is always wrong.

## C2. Usury: Vix pervenit and modern interest

What was taught. Benedict XIV taught that it is unjust to charge for a loan just because it is a loan, but that other reasons, such as risk or lost profit, can justify a charge, and that money can be honestly invested.

## C6. Providentissimus Deus, Divino afflante Spiritu and Dei Verbum

What was taught. Leo XIII taught that Scripture, inspired by God, contains no error at all. Pius XII told scholars to pay attention to literary forms. The Second Vatican Council taught that Scripture teaches without error the truth God wanted written for our salvation.

## D2. Honorius I, Constantinople III and Vatican I

What was taught. Honorius wrote of one will and tried to quiet the dispute over operations. Constantinople Three condemned him and defined two natural wills and operations in Christ.

## D5. Baptism, original sin and the limbo hypothesis

What was taught. The Church teaches that original sin is real and baptism is necessary. Florence taught that those who die in original sin alone are deprived of heaven. By what authority. Conciliar definitions; a papal censure; a study by a theological commission.

What is permanent, and what was a determination for its time. Permanent: original sin and the necessity of baptism. Never defined: limbo. Hope, not certainty: the salvation of these infants.
